# Phase 2: Embedding Model Comparison

This report evaluates whether `BAAI/bge-base-en-v1.5` provides sufficient retrieval quality improvements over `BAAI/bge-small-en-v1.5` to justify its higher computational cost.

## Methodology
- **Dataset:** 472 chunks generated by the hybrid local semantic splitter (Phase 1).
- **Models:** `BAAI/bge-small-en-v1.5` vs `BAAI/bge-base-en-v1.5`.
- **Queries:** 8 specific sports science questions across the nutrition and training domains.
- **Hardware:** Local CPU execution within the project sandbox.

## Latency Comparison

| Model | Parameters | Embedding Time (472 chunks) | Relative Speed |
|---|---|---:|---|
| `bge-small-en-v1.5` | 33M | ~14.5 seconds | 1.0x |
| `bge-base-en-v1.5` | 109M | ~46.1 seconds | ~3.1x Slower |

*Note: The time reflects encoding the entire document corpus. While single-query inference at runtime will be much faster, the 3x latency multiplier remains consistent.*

## Retrieval Quality (Precision@3)

We ran the 8 target queries against both embedded indices and manually evaluated the top 3 chunks (Precision@3).

### Key Observations
1. **High Overlap:** For 5 out of the 8 queries (e.g., *training frequency per muscle group per week*, *rest period recommendations*), both models successfully retrieved the exact same top chunks from the ACSM guidelines and Schoenfeld meta-analyses.
2. **Creatine Protocol:** For *"creatine loading protocol and dosing"*, `bge-small` successfully retrieved the exact section discussing the "4-6 weeks for creatine stores to return to baseline" and maintenance dosing. `bge-base` ranked the paper's front-matter/abstract as its top hit, which is less precise.
3. **Protein Intake:** For *"optimal protein intake per meal"*, `bge-small` ranked the ISSN 20-40g dose recommendation as the #1 hit. `bge-base` ranked it #2, putting a more generic weight-loss protein review at #1. 

## Conclusion & Decision

**Winner: `BAAI/bge-small-en-v1.5`**

There is **no evidence** that `bge-base-en-v1.5` improves retrieval precision for this specific sports science corpus. In fact, `bge-small` performed slightly better at surfacing dense, protocol-specific chunks rather than generic paper abstracts.

Given that `bge-small` is just as accurate (if not better) and **over 3x faster** on CPU, we will strictly use `BAAI/bge-small-en-v1.5` for the remainder of the RAG pipeline implementation.
